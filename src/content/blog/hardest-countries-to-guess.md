Some Globle rounds fall in three guesses. Others drag past a dozen while you stab blindly at the
map. The difference is almost always the answer itself: a handful of countries are genuinely brutal
to pin down. This article ranks the **15 hardest countries to guess in
[Globle Unlimited](/)** and, more usefully, explains *why* each is hard and how to crack it
faster next time.

The ranking is not a matter of opinion. Every country in the game's 176-country pool gets the same
difficulty score the [daily answer page](/globle-answer-today/) prints, and the fifteen below are the
top of that list. The method is at the end of the article.

## Why some countries are so much harder

In Globle you find the answer by reading distance. That breaks down in two situations. **Small
countries** are easy to skip past: the heat climbs as you approach, but the target is a speck you
can overshoot by a whole country. And **countries with few land neighbours** give you nothing to
walk in from. A big continental country is surrounded by near-misses that each read 0 km; an island
has open water on every side, so even a good guess still reads hundreds of kilometres out.

The hardest answers combine both, which is why the list below is mostly islands.

## The 15 hardest countries, ranked

### 1. Trinidad and Tobago
At 5,130 km² it is the third-smallest country in the pool, and it has no land border at all. It sits
just off Venezuela's north-east coast, so a Venezuela guess reads very close while the answer itself
stays hidden. Once the south-east Caribbean is glowing, try it before the larger islands to its
north.

### 2. French Southern and Antarctic Lands
The pool's entry is the Kerguelen Islands, alone in the far southern Indian Ocean. Nothing in the
game sits within thousands of kilometres, so every guess reads far until you name it directly. If
Madagascar, South Africa and Australia all come back cold, think of it.

### 3. Puerto Rico
A US territory east of Hispaniola, smaller than 9,000 km² and with no land neighbours. The
Dominican Republic is the guess that finds it: when that reads only about a hundred kilometres away,
look east.

### 4. Cyprus
An island in the eastern Mediterranean, south of Turkey and west of Syria and Lebanon. The game
splits it in two, and the Republic of Cyprus is the larger, southern part. Guessing
[N. Cyprus](#6-n-cyprus) when the answer is Cyprus reads 0 km, because the two share a border.

### 5. Jamaica
South of Cuba and west of Haiti. Cuba is the reading that gives it away: a hot Cuba with the
answer somewhere to its south almost always means Jamaica.

### 6. N. Cyprus
At 3,355 km² this is the second-smallest entry in the pool. It covers the northern third of Cyprus
and has one land neighbour, the Republic of Cyprus. Players rarely think of it because it is not
widely recognised as a country, which is exactly why it is worth remembering.

### 7. Falkland Islands
In the South Atlantic, about 400 km off the coast of Argentina. Argentina and Chile read warm,
but neither is the answer, and there is nothing else nearby to step to.

### 8. Vanuatu
A chain of islands in Melanesia, east of Australia and north-east of New Caledonia. The south-west
Pacific holds several small island answers close together, so a warm reading there needs a careful
second guess.

### 9. Brunei
Only 5,765 km², on the north coast of Borneo, with Malaysia as its one land neighbour. A Malaysia
guess reads 0 km and looks like a near-win, which is the trap: the answer is the small piece of
coast Malaysia wraps around.

### 10. Bahamas
A spray of islands north of Cuba and south-east of Florida. Large on the map, small on land, and
with no land border to read.

### 11. Fiji
Further east into the Pacific than Vanuatu. When Vanuatu and New Caledonia both read warm but not
hot, keep going east.

### 12. Gambia
A thin strip along the Gambia River, surrounded by Senegal on three sides. Senegal reads 0 km, so a
hot Senegal that is not the answer almost always means Gambia.

### 13. New Caledonia
A French territory in the south-west Pacific, east of Australia. Australia reads warm, at about 1,300 km, but the
answer is the long island off its east coast, not the continent.

### 14. Qatar
A peninsula in the Persian Gulf whose only land neighbour is Saudi Arabia. The Gulf is crowded with
small coastal states, so a warm reading there can hide any of several.

### 15. Timor-Leste
The eastern half of the island of Timor, with Indonesia as its one land neighbour. Indonesia reads
0 km, which tells you that you are next to the answer but not which way.

## Patterns in the hardest answers

Look down the list and the same themes repeat:

- **Caribbean islands** take four places: Trinidad and Tobago, Puerto Rico, Jamaica and the Bahamas.
- **The south-west Pacific** takes three more: Vanuatu, Fiji and New Caledonia.
- **One-neighbour countries** fill most of the rest: Brunei, Gambia, Qatar, Timor-Leste and
  N. Cyprus each have a single land border, and that neighbour reads 0 km whenever you guess it.

If you are wondering why Nauru, Tuvalu or San Marino are not here: they are too small for the
world map Globle Unlimited plays on, so they are not in its 176-country pool. The original Globle
does include micro-states like these, and they are harder still.

## How to beat the hard ones

When a round will not crack, change tactics:

1. **Suspect the islands.** If your warmest guesses are still hundreds of kilometres out and the
   heat refuses to climb, the answer is probably an island. Start with the Caribbean and the
   south-west Pacific.
2. **Treat a 0 km neighbour as a pointer.** When a guess reads 0 km but is not the answer, the
   answer touches it. List the small countries that border it and try those first.
3. **Remember the enclaves.** Lesotho inside South Africa, Gambia inside Senegal, Brunei inside
   Malaysian Borneo — these only appear if you actively think of them.
4. **Use the kilometre reading.** On tiny targets the colour stalls, but the
   [closest-border distance](/how-to-play/) keeps ticking down, so trust the number.

The reliable cure for all of this is exposure: the more countries you have actually guessed, the
fewer can ambush you. A few rounds of [Globle Unlimited](/) a day will turn today's impossible
answer into tomorrow's easy one. For more on openings and triangulation, see our
[Globle strategy guide](/blog/globle-tips-strategy/), and if a daily round defeats you, the
[answer page](/globle-answer-today/) is always there.

## How the ranking is calculated

Each of the 176 countries gets a score from two measured inputs, weighted 65/35:

- **Size:** its rank by land area within the pool, from largest (easiest) to smallest (hardest).
- **Neighbours:** its number of land borders, capped at eight. No neighbours is hardest.

That is the same formula the answer page uses for its 1–5 difficulty rating, and all fifteen
countries above score the maximum 5. It does not measure isolation directly, which is why remote
but larger countries such as New Zealand (60th) and Madagascar (92nd) rank far lower.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the hardest country to guess in Globle Unlimited?**
Trinidad and Tobago scores highest: it is one of the three smallest countries in the pool and has
no land neighbours, so there is no cluster of near-misses to walk in from.

**How do you guess island countries in Globle?**
When your warmest guesses stay hundreds of kilometres out and the heat will not climb, switch to
naming island nations directly — start with the Caribbean and the south-west Pacific, then close in
using the kilometre distance rather than colour alone.
